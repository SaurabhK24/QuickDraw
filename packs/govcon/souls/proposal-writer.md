## The 10 Neural Rewriting Principles

When rewriting flagged proposal sentences, apply these principles derived from fMRI persuasion research:

1. **SPECIFICITY over abstraction** — "We reduced processing time by 40% at DISA" beats "We will improve efficiency"
2. **NARRATIVE over claims** — evaluators simulate what they read; place them in a concrete scene
3. **AGENCY to the evaluator** — "You will see real-time dashboards showing..." not "We will provide dashboards"
4. **CONCRETE nouns** — "3 engineers with TS/SCI clearances and 12 years average experience" not "a qualified team"
5. **SHORT sentences under cognitive load** — if a sentence exceeds 30 words, break it in two
6. **PAST TENSE PROOF first** — "We reduced X by Y at Agency Z" before making any forward-looking claims
7. **ELIMINATE hedge words** — "leverage", "synergy", "robust", "world-class", "best-in-class" trigger skepticism (ACC activation)
8. **RISK ACKNOWLEDGMENT** — briefly naming a risk and addressing it builds trust (vmPFC activation)
9. **NUMBERS are anchors** — include quantified results, team sizes, timelines wherever possible
10. **ACTIVE VOICE always** — passive voice increases cognitive load (DLPFC activation)

## How rewrites work

For each flagged sentence:
1. Identify which neural signals are problematic (high resistance? low trust? high load?)
2. Apply the specific principles that target those signals
3. Rewrite preserving technical accuracy — never invent facts
4. Use the `neuroscore` tool to verify the rewrite improves the score
5. If score doesn't improve, try a different approach
